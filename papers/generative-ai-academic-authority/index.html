Conversion Without Redistribution: Generative AI and Academic Authority in International Publishing

Lijing Liu · Maria Elisabeth Teixeira Pereira

Preprints.org · 2026-07-07

Original paper / full textDownload BibTeX

Research overview

This preprint examines whether the early post-ChatGPT period is associated with changes in global academic participation, authorship leadership and recognition. It uses OpenAlex metadata and distinguishes easier conversion of existing research capacity into manuscripts from redistribution of research resources and recognition.

Original abstract

The release of ChatGPT on 30 November 2022 renewed expectations that generative artificial intelligence might democratize international academic publishing by lowering linguistic and textual barriers. This article develops a narrower account: conversion without redistribution. Generative AI may reduce the cost of converting existing research capacity into internationally legible manuscripts, but it does not directly redistribute data, funding, networks, journal access, or cumulative reputation. Using OpenAlex metadata, the study combines a 51,840-work authorship sample for 2019–2024 with a balanced panel of 13,860 country-subfield-year cells for 2017–2025. Pooled work-level difference-in-differences estimates are positive across participation, leadership, and recognition outcomes. Annual diagnostics, however, show a more selective pattern: the disadvantaged-country first-author share increased by 3.67 percentage points in 2023 and 2.85 points in 2024 relative to the 2022 high-versus-low-exposure difference, whereas most participation, corresponding-author, fractional-author, and high-citation intervals include zero in at least one post year. After restoring 5,385 zero-output panel cells, aggregate triple-difference estimates remain small and window-sensitive. An apparent post-ChatGPT advantage for higher-capacity disadvantaged countries disappears after accounting for a differential trend already visible before 2022. The evidence therefore does not identify a causal democratization effect. Its most defensible implication is narrower: early post-ChatGPT change is visible in one dimension of authorship leadership, but not as broad redistribution of global scientific output or recognition.

Key findings

  • The work-level sample covers 51,840 works; the balanced panel covers 13,860 country–subfield–year cells.
  • Annual diagnostics identify a more selective pattern in disadvantaged-country first authorship, rather than broad and stable gains across participation and recognition.

Scope and limitations

The evidence does not identify a causal democratization effect of generative AI. This is a preprint and has not been peer reviewed.
